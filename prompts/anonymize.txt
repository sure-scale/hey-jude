<task>
Analyze the supplied text and identify all entities that could be personally
identifying information (PII). For each entity, decide the appropriate action.

<instructions>
For each entity you find, classify it and decide an action:

Actions:
- "replace": Real PII. Replace with a GENERIC placeholder that preserves the
  entity's structural role but not its identity. Use the broadest category
  that still lets the task be answered.
  Examples: "Microsoft" → "COMPANY_01", "Dr. Sarah Chen" → "PERSON_01",
  "California" → "REGION_01", "john@acme.com" → "person_01@company_01.com"
- "keep": Not PII. Legal defined terms ("the Purchaser", "the Agreement"),
  generic roles, or terms that are structural rather than identifying.
- "generalize": Partially sensitive. Replace with a broad category that
  preserves useful context. "123 Main St, San Francisco" → "ADDRESS_01"

Rules:
1. Reuse existing mappings for consistency. If "Microsoft" already maps to
   "COMPANY_01", use that same mapping.
2. Use the BROADEST category that still supports the task, never the narrowest.
   A narrow category label is itself an identifier: combined with the visible
   context (named competitors, locations, dates, dollar figures, industry
   terms) it re-identifies the entity. Prefer "COMPANY_01" over
   "INVESTMENT_BANK_01"; prefer "PERSON_01" over "CARDIOLOGIST_01". Only keep a
   narrower category when the task genuinely cannot be answered without it.
3. Real company and organization names are ALWAYS PII — replace them even when
   they also serve as defined terms. A company is PII even if the contract
   defines it as "the Company". Only generic structural labels like
   "the Purchaser", "the Seller", "the Agreement" should be kept.
4. Emit ALL surface-form variants of the same entity as separate entries.
   For people: full name, last name only, title+last name (e.g. "Mr./Ms./Dr.").
   For organizations: full legal name, common short name, abbreviations.
   E.g. "International Business Machines Corporation" → also emit "IBM" and
   any standalone "International Business Machines" found in the text.
   Each variant gets the same replacement placeholder.
5. Numbered placeholders must be unique per category. If PERSON_01 exists,
   next person is PERSON_02. Multiple surface forms of the same entity share
   one placeholder.
6. Catch both numeric and written-out forms of the same value.
   If a dollar amount appears as digits and as words, emit both.
7. Assess overall sensitivity: "low" if replacements alone prevent
   identification, "high" if structural patterns could still de-anonymize
   (famous entities, unique facts, named relationships that survive masking).
8. Identifiability self-check. Before finalizing each placeholder AND its
   descriptor, ask: "Given this placeholder, its descriptor, and the visible
   context (named competitors, locations, dates, dollar figures, product
   lines, industry terms), could a knowledgeable reader name the real entity?"
   If yes, broaden the category until they cannot:
   INVESTMENT_BANK_01 → FINANCIAL_FIRM_01 → COMPANY_01. This applies doubly to
   famous entities whose scale, product line, or location is itself
   identifying.
9. context_descriptors follow the same broadest-category rule. A descriptor
   exists only to keep the text readable for the downstream model — give the
   minimum needed ("a company", "a senior executive") and never a
   distinguishing detail ("the largest US cloud provider", "a Seattle-based
   retailer", "a $2T-market-cap firm"). Do not restate the placeholder's
   category verbatim; if the placeholder already conveys the role, omit the
   descriptor entirely.
10. Quasi-identifier generalization (sensitivity "high" only). A precise figure
    that is not itself PII can still re-identify an entity in combination: an
    exact revenue or deal value, a specific founding or filing date, a unique
    headcount or market share. When sensitivity is "high", generalize such
    precise quantities (action "generalize") to a band that still supports the
    task — "$2.1 billion" → "approximately $2 billion", "March 15, 2024" → "in
    early 2024" — unless the exact value is essential to answer the request.
    Never drop the quantity entirely; preserve its analytic role.
