identifying information (PII). For each entity, decide the appropriate action.

<instructions>
For each entity you find, classify it and decide an action:

Actions:
- "replace": Real PII. Replace with a semantic placeholder that preserves the
  entity's role without identifying it.
  Examples: "Microsoft" → "SOFTWARE_COMPANY_01", "Dr. Sarah Chen" → "PERSON_01",
  "California" → "US_WEST_COAST", "john@acme.com" → "person_01@company_01.com"
- "keep": Not PII. Legal defined terms ("the Purchaser", "the Agreement"),
  generic roles, or terms that are structural rather than identifying.
- "generalize": Partially sensitive. Replace with a broader category that
  preserves useful context. "123 Main St, San Francisco" → "URBAN_ADDRESS_01"

Rules:
1. Reuse existing mappings for consistency. If "Microsoft" already maps to
   "SOFTWARE_COMPANY_01", use that same mapping.
2. Semantic placeholders should reflect the entity's domain/category, not just
   its type. "Goldman Sachs" → "INVESTMENT_BANK_01", not "COMPANY_01".
3. Real company and organization names are ALWAYS PII — replace them even when
   they also serve as defined terms. A company is PII even if the contract
   defines it as "the Company". Only generic structural labels like
   "the Purchaser", "the Seller", "the Agreement" should be kept.
4. Emit ALL surface-form variants of the same entity as separate entries.
   For people: full name, last name only, title+last name (e.g. "Mr./Ms./Dr.").
   For organizations: full legal name, common short name, abbreviations.
   E.g. "International Business Machines Corporation" → also emit "IBM" and
   any standalone "International Business Machines" found in the text.
   Each variant gets the same replacement placeholder.
5. Numbered placeholders must be unique per category. If PERSON_01 exists,
   next person is PERSON_02. Multiple surface forms of the same entity share
   one placeholder.
6. Catch both numeric and written-out forms of the same value.
   If a dollar amount appears as digits and as words, emit both.
7. Assess overall sensitivity: "low" if replacements alone prevent
   identification, "high" if structural patterns could still de-anonymize.
